## E1 — to IFSCA, Division of Investment Funds

**Status: UNSENT as at 11 Aug 2026. Sender: UNASSIGNED.**

**What it settles.** Whether IFSCA already runs arithmetic and vocabulary checks on an FME quarterly
return when it is received. If it does, the filer-side product is a convenience rather than a gap, and the
research result — the residue set, the qualification gate, the coverage declaration — becomes the
headline instead.

**What changes with the answer.** Phase priority only. P9, P10 and P11 rise; P14 and P15 fall. **Not one
line of architecture.**

**Exact text to send:**

> Subject: Validation of the FME quarterly reporting format on receipt
>
> Dear Division of Investment Funds,
>
> I am researching automated consistency checking of prescribed regulatory reporting formats, using the
> published Reporting Format for Fund Management Entities (version dated 02/01/2026) as the worked
> example. The work is academic and unfunded, and no filer data is involved — only the blank template as
> published on ifsca.gov.in.
>
> Two questions, if they can be answered:
>
> 1. When an FME quarterly return is received, are automated checks applied to it — for example, that
>    cross-sheet carried values agree with their source, that row and column totals reconcile, or that
>    dropdown answers fall within the template's own permitted vocabularies?
> 2. Approximately how often is a filing returned to the entity for correction, and what are the most
>    common reasons?
>
> I ask because the published template's own scheme-count total is computed by three COUNTIF expressions
> matching five patterns, while the governing data validation permits nine scheme types — so a filing
> declaring a Special Situation Fund or a Family Investment Fund appears to be under-counted by the
> template itself. I would rather understand the existing process than describe a gap that is not there.
>
> Thank you for your time.

## E1b — to a fund administrator serving GIFT IFSC (KFin / CAMS / Basiz)

**Status: UNSENT as at 11 Aug 2026. Sender: UNASSIGNED.**
**Live-or-retired: LIVE, but demoted.**

The NAV-error wedge was deleted with five measured reasons — decisively, the string `NAV` does not appear
in any XML part of any of the nine prescribed workbooks (MEASURED, positive controls passed: `Scheme` 123,
`USD Million` 31, `Portfolio` 25, `valuation` 6). So this question no longer decides the product. It is
retained because it is the cheapest way to learn whether a written house tolerance exists anywhere in this
market, which is framing the pitch may want and evidence the residue discussion can use.

**Exact question to put:**

> Does your firm operate a written internal tolerance for NAV calculation error — a threshold below which
> a difference is not treated as an error requiring correction or investor compensation — and if so, is it
> expressed as a percentage of NAV, and does it vary by fund type?
>
> For context: CSSF Circular 24/856 specifies such thresholds by fund class for Luxembourg funds, and its
> predecessor 02/77 did so from 2002. I have been unable to locate an equivalent published threshold for
> GIFT IFSC funds and want to know whether one exists in practice rather than in publication.
